Cuts and Cut Capacity

The capacity of a cut is the sum of the capacities of edges crossing from the source side to the sink side.

Worked Example
Find the capacity of Cut A and Cut B above.
Cut A only has edges that run forwards, so its capacity is 1 + 7 = 8.
Cut B has a backwards edge (B to A), so the 3 is ignored. Its capacity is 2 + 4 + 7 = 13.

Things to Note

Tip A cut must separate the source from the sink — check there is no way "around" the cut. Cuts do not need to be straight lines; they can curve around vertices.
